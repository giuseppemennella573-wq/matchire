What an AI booking assistant should (and shouldn't) do for an event vendor

By Giuseppe Mennella, founder of Matchire.ai · Last updated: 13 November 2026

In short

A good AI booking assistant quotes only from your real price list, asks for the event details before it quotes, holds and releases dates on time, and sends anything unusual to you for approval. It should keep your prices private from other vendors, work on the channels your clients already use (WhatsApp and email), let you take over any conversation, and be clear about where your data goes.

Why this matters

An assistant that replies to your clients speaks for your business. A wrong price, a promise you didn't make or a date you can't honour costs more than a slow reply. So before you choose any tool, check what it does when it isn't sure, not just how fast it is.

The checklist

1. It quotes only from your real price list

Every price should come from the packages, add-ons and rules you've set up. If an enquiry asks for something that isn't on your list, the assistant should say so or ask you, never guess or invent a price. Ask to see how it handles extra hours and add-ons that aren't priced.

2. It asks before it quotes

A price without the date and at least one more detail (guest count, venue or hours) is rarely right. A good assistant asks first, briefly, then quotes. This also stops price-shoppers collecting your full rate card.

3. It holds dates and releases them on time

When it quotes, it should hold the date for a set time, tell the planner when the hold ends, and release the date automatically if they don't confirm. Otherwise you end up with dates "held" for people who disappeared weeks ago.

4. It flags unusual requests for you to approve

Custom work, odd venues, discount requests and anything off your price list should come to you before a reply goes out. Look for a clear approval step, and a way to choose which kinds of replies can go out on their own.

5. It keeps your prices private

Your rates are your business. If the tool connects you with other vendors, check exactly what they can see. Other vendors should never see your rate card unless you choose to share it.

6. It works on WhatsApp and email

Event enquiries in Singapore usually arrive on WhatsApp and email. The assistant should work on your existing number and inbox, so planners don't have to change how they contact you.

7. You can take over any conversation

Some clients want to talk to you. You should be able to step in at any time, reply yourself, and have the assistant stop sending messages in that chat until you hand it back.

8. It's clear about your data and backups

Ask who owns your client conversations and price lists, whether they're ever sold or shared, who at the company can open your account, and whether backups are encrypted. You should get plain answers.

Questions to ask any vendor of these tools

  • Where does every price in a reply come from? What happens if it isn't on my list?
  • What does it ask before it sends a price?
  • How long are dates held, and are they released automatically?
  • Which replies need my approval, and can I change that?
  • Can other businesses on the platform see my prices?
  • Does it use my existing WhatsApp number and email, and who sets that up?
  • How do I take over a conversation, and does the assistant stop when I do?
  • Who owns my data? Is it sold or shared? Who can open my account, and is that logged?
  • Are backups encrypted?
  • What does it say to a client when it isn't sure?

If a vendor can't answer these clearly, that tells you something too.
