How to reply to event enquiries in Singapore (and stop losing bookings to slow replies)

By Giuseppe Mennella, founder of Matchire.ai · Last updated: 13 November 2026

In short

Reply quickly, and with a real answer. Before you quote, get the event date plus one more detail: guest count, venue or hours. Then send one clear message that confirms the date, asks at most one question, gives the price, states the deposit and how long you'll hold the date, and says what happens next. Follow up once about 24 hours later, and send a last call before the hold ends.

Why reply speed matters

Planners rarely message just one vendor. When someone is organising a birthday, a wedding or a company dinner, they usually send the same enquiry to several photobooths, DJs, AV companies or florists at once. Then they go with whoever gives them a clear, real answer first.

That means a slow reply often loses the booking before you've even seen the message. It isn't about being the cheapest. It's about being the first vendor who makes the planner's life easy: here's the price, here's what's included, here's how to book.

The hard part is that the busiest vendors are the slowest to reply. If you're running a booth at an event on Saturday night, you can't answer the three enquiries that arrived during the speeches. So it helps to have a reply you can send in a minute, and a simple process for the rest.

Get two details before you quote

A price without context is just a number for the planner to compare. Before you send one, make sure you know:

  1. The date. Without it, you can't check availability, and you can't tell whether weekday or peak pricing applies.
  2. One more detail that changes the price: the guest count, the venue or the number of hours.

If the enquiry already has both, quote straight away. If something is missing, ask for it in your first reply, and ask only for what you need. One short question gets answered. A list of six questions often doesn't.

This also protects you from people who are only collecting price lists. Someone with a real event will happily tell you the date. Someone who just wants your rates for comparison often won't.

A reply structure that works

Keep the same order every time. It's quicker to write, and planners can find what they need at a glance.

  1. Greeting. Use their name if you have it, and thank them for the enquiry.
  2. Confirm the date. Say clearly that the date is available (check first).
  3. One question, if you still need something, such as the venue or the start time.
  4. The price. An exact quote from your packages if you have the details, or a clear range if you don't. Say what's included.
  5. Deposit and hold terms in SGD. How much secures the date, and how long you'll hold it for them.
  6. A clear next step. Tell them exactly how to confirm.
Example reply
Hi [name], thanks for your message! [date] is available for us.

For [guest count] guests over [hours] hours, our [package] is [S$ price], including [what's included].

A deposit of [S$ deposit] secures the date. I'm holding [date] for you until [hold end date].

To confirm, just reply "Yes" and I'll send the payment details.

Always put the deposit in dollars, not just as a share of the total. "A deposit of S$[amount]" is clearer than making the planner do the maths.

When the date is taken

"Sorry, we're fully booked" ends the conversation. Instead, give the planner a way forward:

  • A different package that's free that day. If your 360 booth is out but your classic booth isn't, offer it with its real price.
  • Nearby dates, if their event is flexible. Name the actual dates you have free.
  • A trusted partner. If you can't help at all, recommend another vendor you know does good work. Some vendors agree a referral fee with partners they trust, so the enquiry still earns them something.

When they say "too expensive"

Don't discount by default. A discount given on the first push teaches planners to always push, and it lowers what you earn on every booking that follows. Try these first:

  • Offer a smaller option from your real price list: fewer hours, a simpler package, or fewer add-ons.
  • Explain what's included. Planners often compare your all-in price with someone else's base price.
  • Suggest a quieter day, if you have lower weekday or daytime rates.

Also notice the difference between "too expensive" and "not sure yet". "Not sure yet" isn't a no. Keep the date held and keep the conversation going.

Follow up once, then a last call

Planners often mean to reply and simply forget. A short, friendly follow-up reminds them without any pressure.

  • About 24 hours after the quote, send one gentle nudge: check they saw it and offer to answer questions.
  • Before the hold ends, send a last call: you're holding the date until a certain time, and they can reply to confirm.
  • Then release the date if you don't hear back, so you can offer it to the next enquiry.

Two messages is enough. More than that starts to feel like pressure, and it rarely changes the answer.

Quick checklist

  • Reply as soon as you can, even if it's just to ask for the date.
  • Get the date plus guest count, venue or hours before quoting.
  • Quote from your real price list and say what's included.
  • State the deposit in S$ and the hold end date.
  • Offer options when the date is taken or the price is too high.
  • Follow up once after about 24 hours, send a last call before the hold ends, then release the date.
